\begin{abstract}
This report uses Sentaurus Process and Sentaurus Device to simulate
the fabrication and electrical characteristics of a silicon-on-insulator
(SOI) nMOSFET. Gate length, silicon-film thickness, annealing
temperature, and channel doping are investigated using transfer
and output characteristics, doping profiles, electrostatic maps,
and energy-band diagrams. Thinner top silicon reduces short-channel
leakage with a modest ON-current penalty, while heavier channel
doping suppresses leakage at the expense of drive current.
The channel-centre band profiles are consistent with depleted
silicon bodies under the specified off-gate, high-drain bias.
\end{abstract}

\section{Objective and methodology}
The objective is to construct a two-dimensional silicon-on-insulator (SOI) nMOSFET in Sentaurus Process (SProcess), characterize it in Sentaurus Device (SDevice), and investigate gate length, top-silicon thickness, activation annealing and channel doping. The required electrical quantities are threshold voltage $V_{\mathrm{TH}}$, subthreshold swing (SS), on-current $I_{\mathrm{ON}}$, off-current $I_{\mathrm{OFF}}$, and drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL). The assignment additionally requires longitudinal and vertical energy-band diagrams.

The baseline process was simulated using the supplied \texttt{SProcess\_baseline.txt} script in Sentaurus Workbench J-2014.09. The project was saved at \texttt{/class3/ay2627/a277012m/STDB/SOI\_nMOSFET} on the university server. The corresponding raw TDR and PLX files remain on that server.
No device metrics are inferred merely from the visible process snapshots.

\paragraph{Two-dimensional current normalization.}
The supplied SDevice command files specify
\texttt{Physics \{ AreaFactor=1.0 \}}. In Sentaurus Device's
two-dimensional convention, an \texttt{AreaFactor} of 1
represents an effective out-of-plane width of $1\,\mu$m.
The terminal current of that simulated slice is therefore
numerically equivalent to the current per micrometre of width
for a uniform 3D device. A current for any other physical
gate width must be scaled by its width in micrometres.
We retain the original simulator-exported current magnitudes
throughout the numerical comparison to avoid inadvertently
applying a second width-scaling factor. This convention follows
the \emph{Synopsys Sentaurus Device User Guide},
which describes the 2D effective-width and
\texttt{AreaFactor} definition; the modified Workbench
decks are assumed to preserve the baseline setting
unless otherwise noted.

\subsection{Fabrication evidence and interpretation}
\paragraph{Initial SOI stack.} The supplied script saves \texttt{soi\_00\_start\_fps.tdr} after depositing the buried oxide and top silicon. The $30$-nm top-silicon layer is separated from the underlying silicon by the $150$-nm buried oxide (BOX). This establishes the SOI body and reduces electrical coupling to the substrate, an essential feature of SOI electrostatic control.

\paragraph{Gate oxidation.} A dry-oxygen step of $800\,^\circ$C for $0.5$ min is used before the polysilicon gate is patterned. The snapshot \texttt{soi\_01\_gateoxide\_fps.tdr} is available. The gate-oxide layer forms the insulating dielectric between the subsequently deposited polysilicon gate and the silicon surface. It enables electrostatic gate coupling while suppressing direct gate-channel conduction. The process conditions are specified from the archived SProcess command file.

\input{sections/part1_gateoxide_profile}
The measured surface-oxide thickness of 1.532 nm is obtained
from the oxide's two region-interface coordinates in the
exported 1D TDR data. The silicon beneath remains about 30 nm
thick and the buried oxide about 150 nm thick.

\evidence{02_extension}{After gate patterning and shallow arsenic extension implantation (\texttt{soi\_02\_extension\_fps.tdr}). The gate masks the channel while shallow doped regions are formed beside its edges.}
\noindent The extension implant uses arsenic with a specified energy of $0.5$ keV followed by a $900\,^\circ$C, $0.1$ s thermal step. The shallow n-type extensions form access regions close to the gated channel and reduce parasitic series resistance. Their junction placement also affects short-channel electrostatics; the subsequent deeper source/drain implant is deliberately offset by the spacers.

\evidence{03_spacer}{Oxide sidewall spacer formation (\texttt{soi\_03\_spacer\_fps.tdr}). Rounded spacer geometry is visible beside the patterned gate.}
\noindent The process deposits oxide isotropically and then etches it anisotropically. The remaining sidewall oxide masks the gate-adjacent silicon during the subsequent deep source/drain implantation, separating the high-dose junctions from the gate edge and helping control overlap capacitance and junction encroachment.

\evidence{04_annealed}{Deep source/drain arsenic implantation and activation anneal (\texttt{soi\_04\_annealed\_fps.tdr}); net active doping is displayed.}
\noindent The deep implant uses arsenic at $15$ keV, followed by annealing at $950\,^\circ$C for $10$ s. The high-dose implant establishes low-resistance n$^+$ source/drain regions, and the anneal electrically activates implanted arsenic while also permitting lateral and vertical diffusion. Deeper or more laterally extended junctions can reduce access resistance but may increase leakage and weaken short-channel control. The strongly doped polysilicon gate can also be visible in a net-doping display and should not be mistaken for the silicon channel.

\evidence{05_final}{Final contact/mesh structure (\texttt{soi\_nmos\_fps.tdr}). This is the structural input prepared for electrical characterization.}
\noindent In the supplied script, the \texttt{contact name=gate ... replace point} operation assigns a gate contact by replacing a point-selected polysilicon region; source, drain and substrate contacts are also defined. The final TDR therefore need not look identical to the earlier gate-poly snapshot. The final structure was inspected in Sentaurus Visual; the contact definitions and refined mesh provide the electrically contacted geometry used by SDevice for bias-dependent current and field calculations.

\subsection{Current visualization and limitations}
The displayed scalar \texttt{NetActive} represents net electrically active doping, with donor and acceptor signs distinguished. It is not the same quantity as free electron concentration. The TDR views provide qualitative fabrication evidence; numerical implant profiles and extracted terminal characteristics must be separately quantified before making performance claims.

The subthreshold swing should be extracted from an appropriately selected approximately linear portion of the semilogarithmic curve:
\[
SS=\left(\frac{d\log_{10}|I_D|}{d V_{GS}}\right)^{-1},
\]
expressed in mV/decade. The fitted numerical dataset gives $SS=63.61$ mV/dec at nominal $V_{DS}=0.1$ V. Specifically, a least-squares fit of $\log_{10}(I_D)$ against $V_{GS}$ over the eight points from $-0.35$ to $0$ V has slope $15.720597$ decade/V and $R^2=0.999999892$. The expression above yields $1000/15.720597=63.6108$ mV/dec. The extracted fit is a nominal simulation result rather than a measurement of physical silicon.
